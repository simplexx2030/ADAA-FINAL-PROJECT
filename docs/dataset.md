## Random, but not *entirely* random

The bulk of the data is randomised — mixed skills, ratings, verification states,
availability and travel radii — so the matching engine has realistic variety to filter.

Three deliberate exceptions:

**1. The random seed is fixed (`random.seed(42)`).**
Running the generator twice produces byte-identical files. Specification section 23 asks
whether the agent gives *consistent* decisions when the underlying data has not changed.
That question is unanswerable if the dataset shifts under it.

**2. Reputation is calculated, never typed in.**
This is the most important property of the dataset. An earlier version wrote
"Suresh: 31 jobs, 4.70 rating" straight into the workers table. It looked convincing until
you opened his profile and found no jobs and no ratings behind it.

Now the generator creates the history first - real jobs, real assignments, real ratings -
and then **counts** it:

- `workers.completed_jobs` is the number of `job_assignments` rows with status `completed`
- `workers.average_rating` is the mean of that worker's rows in `ratings`
- `crews.rating` is the mean of ratings targeting the crew
- `reliability_score` is a stated formula over attendance, rating and no-shows

Individual ratings are generated in balanced pairs around each target, so the mean is exact
by construction. Anyone can recompute a headline number from the tables and get the same
answer. That is what "the database is the source of truth" means in practice.

`attendance_rate` is the exception: it is a day-level figure (days present divided by days
scheduled) and is not derivable from job-level assignment rows, so it is stated directly.

**3. The demonstration characters are hard-coded.**
Purely random data would break the six demo scenarios. These records are fixed:

| Who | Fixed values | Needed by |
|---|---|---|
| Ravi Crew (`RAVI01`) | 7 active members, 6 of them available verified masons, rating 4.80, 32 jobs | §14, §15 |
| Suresh (`W001`) | 31 completed jobs, 4.70 rating, 96% attendance, verified mason, member of Ravi Crew | §16, §17 |
| Raju (`W003`) | independent mason, 18 jobs, 4.50 rating | §14 |
| Mahesh (`W002`) | leader of Mahesh Crew (`M001`) | §13 |
| Kumar (`W004`) | helper in Ravi Crew, 15 jobs, 4.40 | §13 |
| `W012`–`W013` | two more independent verified masons, free tomorrow | §14 |
| Bhaskar (`W014`) | left Ravi Crew six months ago; kept 27 jobs and a 4.50 rating | §17 |

Bhaskar matters more than he looks. Scenario 5 asks what happens to a worker's reputation
when they leave a crew. Because Bhaskar actually left Ravi Crew, that question is answered
by opening his profile rather than by describing a policy: his 27 jobs, his 4.50 rating and
his verified masonry skill are all still his, and the membership row survives with
`status='left'` and an end date.

## Two places where this differs from the specification

**1. The 8-mason example double-counts Suresh.**

Specification §14 gives the expected answer as:

```text
Ravi Crew — 6 workers
Suresh    — 1 worker
Raju      — 1 worker
Total     = 8
```

But §13's `workers.csv` lists Suresh as a member of `RAVI01`, and §15 says Ravi's crew has
*six available verified masons*. If the crew contributes 6 and Suresh is also counted
individually, Suresh is counted twice.

This dataset resolves it by keeping Suresh inside Ravi Crew, as §16 requires ("Current
crew: Ravi Crew"). So the composition the engine will produce is:

```text
Ravi Crew — 6 workers  (includes Suresh)
Raju      — 1 worker
+ one more independent mason
Total     = 8
```

The specification itself says the result must come from the dataset (§14), so a correct,
internally consistent answer is preferred over reproducing an example that contradicts
itself.

**2. Mahesh is not in Ravi Crew.**

§13's `workers.csv` puts Mahesh in `RAVI01`, while its `crews.csv` makes him the leader of
`M001`, "Mahesh Crew". He cannot be both. He is the leader of Mahesh Crew here.

## One addition to the schema

`workers.attendance_rate` is not in specification §9.1, but §16's demonstration requires
"Attendance: 96%", and §9.11 expects an `attendance_factor` in the independence
assessment. Attendance could be derived from `job_assignments`, but storing it directly is
simpler to read and to explain, which the specification asks for (Rule E). It can be made
derived later without changing the API.

## Regenerating

```bash
backend/.venv/Scripts/python backend/scripts/generate_data.py
backend/.venv/Scripts/python backend/scripts/seed_database.py
```

The seeder drops and recreates every table, so this is always safe to repeat while
prototyping. It is **not** safe once there is real data.
